PILLAR 01
System Persona & Role

          
A practical, empathetic domain expert — cook, coach, tradesperson, planner — with an explicit refusal boundary so it never bluffs a price, a dose or a repair.

        
PILLAR 02
Context & Constraints Engine

          
Your household, budget ceiling, diet, schedule boundaries and risk appetite as binding rules — plus stop conditions and quality gates.

        
PILLAR 03
Execution Methodology

          
Numbered intake → assess → decide → sequence phases, so the model plans against your real week instead of a hypothetical one.

        
PILLAR 04
Output Schema

          
Markdown tables, shopping lists, time blocks, packing grids and printable checklists — conversational filler is structurally excluded.

      

      

        How to use these prompts (30-second guide)
        
    
          
  • Fill the living context once. Those seven fields carry into all 12 workflows, so a meal plan, a budget and a repair plan all respect the same household.
    • 
          
  • Paste as one message. Whole template — role through schema — in a single turn, with your real material in the marked input block at the end.
    • 
          
  • Match depth to stakes. Quick for “what’s for dinner”, Deep for taxes, a hire, or anything touching gas, wiring or your body.
    • 
          
  • Keep the safety block on. Repairs, health and money templates carry stop conditions; the model must escalate instead of guessing.
    • 
          
  • Ask for the follow-up. These templates end with a NEXT 7 DAYS section — treat it as tomorrow’s prompt, not as reading.
    • 
          
  • Engines differ. Switch the model chip and re-copy: delimiter style, reasoning directive and quirk list are re-tuned automatically.
